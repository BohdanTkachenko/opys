# License split: Apache-2.0 core, AGPL server components

## Context

The whole workspace is Apache-2.0 today. Under [ADR-0053 — Everything open source; sell connectivity, hosting, and convenience](ADR-0053.md) the business *is* the
hosted service, and a 100%-permissive relay/server means anyone can stand up a
competing commercial host of our exact product the week pricing is announced.
The standard defense (Grafana, MinIO) is copyleft on the server-side
components while the tool stays permissive: still 100% open source, still
freely self-hostable — AGPL only forces commercial rehosts to publish their
modifications.

## Decision

Proposed, not yet decided: keep `opys` (CLI), `opys-engine`, backends, and the
skill under Apache-2.0 (maximal adoption surface for the tool every agent
runs); license the forthcoming server, relay, and hosted-web components under
AGPL-3.0. Decide before the first `opys-server` release — relicensing later
is far harder than choosing at birth (every outside contribution locks the
license in further absent a CLA).

## Consequences

- Contributions to AGPL crates need either a CLA/DCO decision or acceptance
  that the license can never change without contributor consent.
- Some corporate users refuse AGPL on the *client* side; the split keeps
  everything they embed or script (CLI/engine) Apache, which is the point.
- If rejected, the fallback is trademark + operational moat only (the
  Ghost approach — Ghost is MIT everywhere and relies on brand and hosting
  quality).
